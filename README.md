# ERP script

A Tampermonkey userscript that makes slow parts of an ERP web app usable, without changing the site.

| Page | Problem (measured 10 Nov 2026) | What the script does |
|---|---|---|
| Work Orders | Downloads 11.6 MB (5,000 + 3,000 orders), draws 6,347 rows (205,000 elements), freezes the tab ~4.9 s | Asks for the newest 500 of each list: 589 rows, 19,000 elements, ~0.2 s. Switch to 1000 or All from the bar at the bottom right; the choice is remembered |
| Approvals | Draws every waiting report (275 rows, 8,300 elements) and redraws them all on each click, ~0.3 s freeze | Shows the first 50 (the rest are only hidden, filters and PDF export still cover all): ~0.05 s. Switch to 100 or All from the bar at the bottom right; the choice is remembered |
| Planning | Downloads 11 MB of network assets (backbone, OLT, ODP…) on every visit. In Rute Tarikan Kabel, DARI / KE Aset each list all 5,095 assets and redraw on every key: 2.1 s to show the section, ~0.13 s a key, ~3 s to open a list | Reuses the assets for 15 minutes (the bar shows how old, with Refresh). Pick a district in the bar and the lists keep that district plus every OLT and ODC (~600): 0.2 s to show, typing smooth (~16 ms a key), 0.2 s to open. Assets with no district are placed by village or nearest asset; poles with no place at all are left out. Pick "All districts" to see everything |
| LDN Radius → Pelanggan search | Asks the server on every search (1–4 s) and matches the typed text as one piece: an extra space, words in another order or a trailing `.` find nobody (0 of 5 in a test) | A **fast search** dropdown under the box: all customers are loaded once (13,520; a page that comes back short is asked for again; about 2 minutes the first time, then kept and refreshed in the background) and searched as you type in ~30–80 ms, any word order, spacing and punctuation ignored, forgiving of spelling (double letters: winna = wina; old spellings: oe = u, dj = j, tj = c; one letter off from 4 letters, two from 9), across name, username, phone, address, village, router and plan (5 of 5 in the same test). ↑↓ and Enter (or a click) put that customer's username into the site's search, so the table shows them |
| Finance → Invoices search | Same as LDN: one server request per search (~2.8 s), the typed text matched as one piece (an extra space, another word order or a trailing `.` find nobody: 0 of 5) | The same **fast search**, for the month the page shows (it follows the month you pick): about 12,000 invoices a month, loaded once (about a minute; the server gives 200 per request, about one request a second). For **Semua Bulan** it loads month by month, newest first, and searches whatever has arrived (a whole year takes about 11 minutes the first time); each month is kept on its own, then searched in ~30–60 ms across invoice number, WinPay ID, name, RADIUS username, contract, village, router, plan and phone. Each result shows status, amount and period. Enter puts the invoice number into the site's search, so the table shows that invoice |
| Idle logout with several tabs open | The ERP logs out after 120 minutes without a click, key, touch or scroll, counted in **each tab on its own**, and a logout in one tab signs out every tab, so a forgotten background tab ends the session you're working in | Real activity in any ERP tab counts for all of them (passed between tabs at most every 30 s). Only real input travels: 120 minutes of doing nothing still logs you out |

The page searches and filters only what it has loaded, so for an older work order choose **All**.

The bar follows the Faust design (Night Ledger: near-black card, bone dot label, graphite segments, blue focus ring). The fast search moves the Faust way too: the box unfolds with a slight un-blur, its label types itself in with a faint blinking cursor, new results rise in one after another (ones that stay don't move), the selection slides between rows, a bone progress line with a light sweep shows loading, and the box folds away on close. All of it is off under the system's reduced-motion setting.

## The Dock

A floating button above the site's own corner button (or **Alt+K**, anywhere in the ERP) opens one search box for customers, pages and tools:

- **Customers**: from the LDN customer list this script keeps (spelling-tolerant, any word order). Each customer opens a card with status (online / offline / isolir), username, phone (copy or WhatsApp), address, area, plan, router, IP, active since, expiry, ONT serial and partner, and the **technicians** for the customer's village from the ERP's own map (HRD → Pemetaan Wilayah Teknisi), by team village lists and by each technician's individual placement (village names compared without spaces). When the village isn't on file or no team covers it, the card says so and goes by the village most of the nearest customers are in, else the area head for the district, else the main village on the customer's router: field team first, then area head (KA …), backbone and office teams, with their members (lead marked); a village no team covers says so. The outage report adds the field team (Tim: …) for its main villages.
- **Pay** (on the card): the customer's unpaid months, asked from the server when the card opens (about 3 s), oldest ticked, with RADIUS recharge. Pick the method, date and note and press **Mark … Lunas** twice: the same request as the ERP's Tandai Lunas Manual, oldest first, with the result on each month. No need to go to Invoices. **Skip older months** (finance manager / admin) pays only the months you tick, for a customer who was away or didn't use the internet: the ERP's own "Lunasi walau ada tagihan lebih tua" exception. The older months stay unpaid, the note must say why (10+ letters), and the ERP logs it as [OVERRIDE tertua-dulu].
- **Open in …**: LDN Radius (on the Pelanggan tab), Invoices, Map Terpadu or Master Pelanggan, with that page's own search filled in when it arrives; Google Maps from the customer's location.
- **Nearby**: everyone within 100 m, 300 m or 1 km of the customer, counted online / offline / isolir, nearest first, on a dark street map (OpenStreetMap's own tiles, darkened; or CARTO's dark map once you paste your own CARTO key under the map: it's kept only in that browser, never in the script; with a key, **CARTO / OSM** under the map switches between the two without losing it, and **Remove key** forgets it), where the status colours stand out, or with **Satellite** the view Peta Terpadu uses (Google hybrid) for finding the house; rings at half and full distance, zoom buttons, full screen (⤢, or **F** with the mouse over the map; F or Esc to go back), and the choice is remembered. Below it, **Same PON**: the customer's OLT and PON port with how many of its modems are offline (from the OLTs' cached lists), a verdict (many down together = the line: PON, splitter or ODP; only this one = the house), its customers outlined on the map, and the offline ones listed to open. Pointing at a dot shows the name and distance and lights up its row; pointing at a row lights up its dot; clicking a dot opens that customer. For checking an area when someone reports an outage.
- **Modems**: type a modem serial (whole or part, e.g. `FHTTC1848B61` or `59000648`) to find it on any of the 46 OLTs (their cached ONT lists, about 11,000 ONTs, kept 2 hours; the first look takes ~6 s), on TR-069 (GenieACS) or in the customer list. Each customer's card has a **Modem** box (found by the SN on file, else by the PPPoE username): model, OLT · PON · ONU, signal (green from −26 dBm, amber to −28, red below) with **Read now** for a live reading from the OLT, and whether it reports to TR-069. For modems on TR-069: **WiFi password** (shown 30 seconds), **Change WiFi** (2,4 / 5 GHz / both, new name and/or password, with a generator). **Reboot** goes through TR-069 when the modem reported in the last 15 minutes, else through the OLT by SN. Changes and reboots need a second press; a modem that's offline or behind NAT gets the change queued, and the box says so. These are the ERP's own calls (ACS / TR-069 and ONT management pages), so nothing touches the MikroTik.
- **Remote ↗** (on the card, in the Modem box, or **R** on a card when you're not typing) opens the modem's own page in one press: it finds the live PPPoE session and opens the modem's page through the ERP's 5-minute access on the RO (logged; any PC). If the ERP can't find the session (customer filed on another RO), it says so. The ERP's access is a port the router removes after 5 minutes; **Keep open** (off by default; switch it on next to the countdown) asks for it again about 30 seconds before it closes, each renewal logged like a press, for at most 30 minutes, and stops as soon as the modem's tab is closed. The 5 minutes is the ERP's deliberate limit and each renewal briefly opens the modem to the internet again, so use it only when a job needs it. If a renewal comes back with another address, the modem's tab is moved there. If the modem isn't online it says so, and reboot still works.
- **Login** in the Modem box: save the modem's admin login once per model, or for a whole brand (type just `zte`), and it shows **Copy user** / **Copy password** on every modem of that model; **Remote** copies the password as it opens the page. Logins are kept only in that browser's storage, unencrypted and never in the script: save them only on your own PC, not a shared one.
- **Modem web page** (in the Modem box, *Find the modem's address*): the modem's IP and the RO its session is really on, read from RADIUS. The ERP's Remote ONU (Dashbor NOC) looks only on the RO in the customer record, and about 1 in 10 online customers are on another RO (old ARJOSARI / TREMAS records), so it finds nothing for them; the Dock says when that's the case, and **Fix RO: set to …** (press twice) corrects the record with the ERP's own Set Router (LDN → Pelanggan), to the router the session is really on (matched by its NAS address and name). That changes the record only (Router column and ERP master), not the PPPoE connection. The ERP's Remote ONU reads its own copy of the customer list, which Set Router doesn't refresh, so Remote only works for that customer once the ERP updates its copy; the Dock says when it still reads the old RO. Where the ERP does find the session: **Test from the RO** (ping, port 80, 8080; ~20 s, read-only) and **Open from anywhere · 5 min** (the ERP's temporary port on the RO, logged, closes itself; press twice), with a countdown. When every port clearly doesn't answer, it says the modem doesn't serve its page on the internet side and points to the TR-069 buttons.
- **Open from anywhere** (3.26.0): the ERP's 5-minute access gives the RO's WireGuard address, which loads only on a PC on the NOC WireGuard. Add the RO's public IP once under Web page (kept in that browser only) and Remote opens the same port on it, so the modem's page loads from any network; the WireGuard link stays beside it. It works when the RO's rule for that port isn't tied to the WireGuard address. While it's open, anyone with the address and port reaches the modem's login page, as with the ERP's link.
- **Cause & dispatch** (Outage radar → a router): the customers down on that router grouped by the PON port their modem is on, each with what the OLT says (LOS = a cut or bent fibre, dying gasp = no power), how much of the port is down, the FAT/ODP most likely at fault (needs Planning's asset list, cached once Planning is opened) and a verdict: *fibre cut on this port's line*, *fibre problem near one FAT*, *power cut*, *many down, cause unknown* or *scattered* (a few modems switched off, not the network). **Make SPK** (highlighted for a real cluster) picks a team that covers those villages (or any other), a type (Maintenance Jaringan Umum by default) and a title naming the router, port and FAT, and saves the same record as Planning's manual form for a team, Bukan proyek, today, after two presses; then it gives a ready WhatsApp message for the team. **Copy for the team** gives that message without an SPK.
- **Down?** (on every customer card): the evidence for "internet mati". *When*: online since (the session's start), or down since — the modem's last TR-069 report where it has one (to the minute), else between the Dock's last reading that saw them online and the first that didn't; reconnects in the last 24 hours and 7 days with their times (4+ a day = unstable). *Who else*: modems offline on the same PON port, customers offline within 300 m, the router's state. *Then*: the modem's OLT, port, status and signal, the session's IP · MAC · NAS, open complaints and arrears. A verdict (isolir; router down; whole port down; neighbours down; only them with critical signal = drop cable; only them with fine signal = modem or its power; unstable; online and steady = inside the house) and **Copy summary** for a ticket or chat. **Check now** reads the sessions again.
- **Drop log**: the ERP keeps no history of when a line went down, so every 15 minutes one ERP tab reads LDN's live session list (6.4 MB, ~1 s) and keeps 7 days of each customer's session starts and last time seen online, in this browser (`erp-fast` → `drops`, ~1.5 MB). Times are only as complete as the hours an ERP tab was open.
- **Bulk SPK** and **Review queue** (Tools, or type "spk" / "review"): open those tools from any page. If you're elsewhere the Dock goes to Planning or Approvals first and opens the tool once the page has loaded.
- **PON board** (Tools, or type "pon" / "odp"): every PON port from the OLTs' cached ONT lists, ranked by modems offline (ports with a quarter or more down, 3 at least, first) or by weak signal, each with the age of its newest reading (some OLTs aren't polled for weeks). Modems listed on 0/0/0 (no real port) aren't ranked. Opening a port shows its modems, offline and weakest first, a verdict (many down together = the line; several critical = connectors or splitter) and its customers on the same street/satellite map as Nearby, fitted to where they are (isolir red, offline white with a red ring for a cut fibre and a dashed one for no power, online green; hover for the name, click to open), with how many of the port's modems could be placed (only those linked to a customer with a saved location).
- **Weak signal** (Tools, or type "signal" / "redaman"): online modems below −28 dBm (critical) or −26 (weak), the ERP's own classes, weakest first, with the PON ports where several are weak at the top: several weak on one port points at a dirty connector or a bad splitter. Readings of 0 or −40 are OLTs saying "no reading" and are left out. Every row opens the customer (or the modem).
- **Outage report** (on a PON port and on a radar router): a ready-to-send message in Indonesian for the technicians (where, how many down of how many, since when, villages, a Google Maps point, the time), with **WhatsApp ↗**, **Copy report**, the affected customers' phone numbers (62…) and a short notice for them.
- **Data health** (Tools, or type "data" / "health"): which numbers to trust. Every OLT's list by age, oldest first, with **Read now** (the ERP's own read-only discovery) and, for stale ones, why the ERP can't reach the OLT (from its own connection test); routers whose session feed is stopped or empty though customers are on file; and **customers on the wrong RO** (the RO on file isn't where their session is), counted by pair, each pair opening its customers, with the whole list as CSV.
- **Map Terpadu** (Go to) opens the Dock's own **Network map** full screen instead of the ERP page: every active customer with a saved location, online from the live session list, with the brief, villages and teams, nearest FAT/ODP, down-for times and replay described under Maps for technicians. The ERP's own page is one button away (**ERP's Peta Terpadu**). Only dots near the view are drawn, and past 1500 the online ones are painted in one go (no name on hover), so the whole network pans smoothly. **SPK** on the map's buttons makes Maintenance SPKs from it: each FAT/ODP with customers down gets a +, click the ones to send a technician to, pick the team (the field team covering the first box's village is chosen for you) and the date, and press **Make** twice. Each picked box is one Maintenance Jaringan Umum SPK at the box's place (the form's own manual SPK with a location), titled "Perbaikan FAT … · 5/8 pelanggan mati", saved all together as Bulk SPK saves them (Bukan proyek). The boxes come from Planning's asset list, so open Planning once in this browser first.
- **Outage radar** (Tools, or type "radar" / "gangguan"): every 5 minutes one ERP tab reads the LDN panel's router feed (sessions open on each router, 20 KB) and keeps 48 hours of it. Each router is compared with its own last two hours: a drop of 10% (at least 8 sessions) is an alert, below 30% is *down*. A router whose feed stopped while its count stays near usual is *no data* (amber): the ERP stopped hearing from it, so the count is frozen and doesn't say whether customers are online; several stopping at the same moment is called out as the accounting link or the ERP's reader rather than outages. While a router has no data, its customers get a hollow grey dot (search, nearby, the card's "Unknown since …") instead of the ERP's frozen online/offline. Alerts put a red dot on the Dock button and on the Outage radar row (the Dock still opens on its menu), with when the dip began; most routers dropping at once is called out as a central problem. Every router has a 24-hour line and, under it, who covers it: the field team(s) and the KA (area head) whose villages hold at least a quarter of its customers, from HRD → Pemetaan Wilayah Teknisi; alerts carry the same line, and an opened router lists each team with its members. Opening one reads the live sessions (6 MB, about a second) to show who's offline right now: count, a map for the technicians (see below), villages with the most offline first (open one for the customers), and how many reconnected in the last 15 minutes (the line coming back). Alerts start about 25 minutes after first use, once there's a usual level to compare with. **Topology** (in the radar's header) takes the network map as text, one line per router and what's below it (`RO UPSTREAM > RO A, RO B, OLT C`), kept in that browser only. A router the ERP's router map can't reach counts as down at once, with the state of its uplink port on the other end (3.27.2): a dead router can't report its sessions ending, so RADIUS keeps them open and the session count alone notices only 15–20 minutes later. Since 3.23.0 the map comes from the ERP's own router map (Network → Topology) on its own, read with the radar, each link pointing away from the core; text pasted there replaces it. Each outage card at a point on the map says how many customers are usually online behind it, and how many are now (3.24.0); Way up's verdict says the same for the link or router that failed, or the OLT's modem count. **Uplinks at peak** (3.25.0): the tab that reads the radar also notes every link's load every 5 minutes (per hour, 14 days, in that browser); the radar lists the links that reach 90% or 75% on two or more days, with the hours they fill up and a 24-hour strip. Way up puts the same pattern on each link, Down?'s summary gets a Way up line, and the Answer says "slow at peak hours" when the customer's uplink fills up at the same hours most days. With it, routers that fail together become one card at the point where it starts: a router down with everything failing behind it ("start there, or its link from …"), or two or more branches failing below a router that is fine or has no customers of its own ("likely at …": its ports, or the router and its uplink). Routers covered by a card aren't repeated as alerts, and a **Network** section shows the map as a tree with each router's state. Feed names are matched to the map by name, by the longest map name they start with (RO PUNUNG NEW → RO PUNUNG), or by a unique first word (RO PACITAN 2116 → RO PACITAN KOTA).
- **Pages**: any ERP page by name, learned from the sidebar as you browse.
- **Home**: customers, online and isolir counts from the kept list, shortcuts, and your recent customers.
- **1× link** (on a customer card): the same as Master Pelanggan's Link 1× Klik. One press makes the customer's one-click login link and the ERP tries to send it on WhatsApp. If its gateway fails, the card shows the link, the login (phone number and password) and **WhatsApp ↗** with the ERP's ready message, to send by hand, plus **Try WhatsApp again**. Needs a phone number on the customer.
- **Bulk Lunas**: paste a list of usernames (into the Dock's search, or search "lunas" / "bayar"), or a WhatsApp chat as copied ("[16.13, 20/11/2026] Sender: username", time or date first: only the message is kept; a line starting with a username keeps only the username, so "budi0101 Oktober" or "budi0101 b November 110rb" is just the customer, and a name with its village has months and amounts taken out, and times, dates and "<Media omitted>" are left out). A line not found, with a choice to make, or found only by a guess gets a search box: type a name, username or phone and pick the customer (from the Dock's customer list, or the ERP's invoices when the list has nothing). Pasting again while it's still looking starts over at once (lines still in the box keep their lookup, removed ones are dropped before they reach the server); with the box unchanged the button stops the lines still waiting. Each one is looked up on the server (all months, about 4 s each, five to ten at a time). Every unpaid invoice is loaded once when Bulk Lunas opens (about a minute for ~10,000, kept 10 minutes), so each username is then found at once; until then each line is searched on its own straight away, with a short list's searches going before the unpaid list (it pauses, since both at once slow the ERP down) and a list of over 40 lines letting it keep loading; names, typos and customers with nothing unpaid are searched as before and shows the customer, what's paid and every unpaid month with its amount, the oldest ticked. Ticking a month ticks the older ones too, since the ERP pays oldest first. A username that isn't exact gets the closest match (left unticked, in amber) or a choice of likely ones; a line with a name and a village ("Jarmo Sumberagung") is found the way the customer search finds it: among that village's customers, the name matched exactly, by its start or by sound (Djarmo = Jarmo), the village's username code (0904) breaking ties; one clear fit is taken (unticked, in amber), several give a choice showing each one's name and village. A customer listed twice is shown once. RADIUS recharge is checked with the ERP's own preview and on where it can be. Pick the method (Tunai, Transfer Bank, Setor Reseller, Setor Teknisi), the payment date and a note, then press **Mark … Lunas** twice. **Latest month only** (finance manager / admin, like the card's Skip older months) ticks each customer's newest month alone and leaves the older ones unpaid; the note must say why (10+ letters) and the ERP logs it as [OVERRIDE tertua-dulu]. It sends the same request as the ERP's Tandai Lunas Manual, one invoice per request, three to six customers at once, more while the ERP answers quickly and fewer when it slows down or fails (each customer's own months one after another, oldest first; if one doesn't go, that customer's newer ones wait), and shows the result on each line (Lunas, recharged, or why the server skipped it). Pressing it sends the list off with its own method, date and note and empties the box: paste the next collector's list while it pays. Each sent list shows its progress and **Stop** (the payments already in progress finish), then the lines that didn't go, with **Try again** (not for one whose answer was lost and couldn't be checked). Lines not found, waiting for a choice or with nothing ticked stay in the box. **Today: … paid · … not yet** at the top lists everyone from today's lists who isn't paid and the lines never found, and puts them back in the box to check again (**Forget the … never found** clears lines that were never found, such as a typo or a chat line read wrong, and keeps what was paid), with what each collector's lists paid (lists with the same method and note added up, customers and rupiah) and **Copy totals** to send; it's kept in this browser for the day.

Apart from paying (the card's Pay and Bulk Lunas) and the outage radar, it never asks the server on its own: it reads what's kept in this browser. Open LDN Radius → Pelanggan once to fill the customer list (the first time takes about 2 minutes), and Invoices once for invoices. After that the customer list is read again every 5 minutes in the background (about 30 s, by one ERP tab at a time and only while it's on screen; the other tabs use its copy), so online/offline and isolir stay current.

Colours everywhere in the Dock and on its maps: online green, isolir red, offline white; red also marks an alarm (critical signal, a port mostly down, stale data) and amber a warning.


### Aktivasi helper

In Technical → Antrean Aktivasi, the ⚡ activation form stays exactly as the ERP made it. While it's open, the Dock button gets a white dot; pressing it opens the activation helper next to the button (press again or × to close), without closing the form:

- **Technician's report:** technician, reported ODP and port, signal, modem.
- **FAT from the technician:** paste what they sent (`pakai fat jimat 4c ya pak, port 3`). It finds the FAT in the database despite chat words and loose spelling, reads the port, and **Use** fills both.
- **FAT/ODP from the report:** matched to the database even when typed loosely (`\FAT LDN NW15`, `FAT 8BBLH`).
- **Nearest FATs/ODPs with free ports** (folded, as a cross-check): measured from the install point, with distance and ports in use. **Use** fills the form's FAT and port fields.
- **OLT:** the form won't activate without one. The helper suggests it from the 25 nearest customers whose modem is on an OLT list (or the same village when there's no install point): each OLT with how many of them, the nearest one's distance and their usual PON. If the modem's SN is already on an OLT list, that OLT is shown instead. **Use** picks it in *Pindai Modem di OLT* and presses its Scan.
- **Nama Pelanggan:** the name as the ERP lists customers, name + village + STARLITE (`BUDI SANTOSO SUKOREJO STARLITE`). **Use** puts it in the form.
- **Username ideas:** always name + village code, in the ERP's own style (`budisantoso0601`). When the plain name is taken, the village name goes in instead of the form's extra number (`budisukor0601` rather than `budis06012`). Each idea is checked against RADIUS as free or taken; **Use** puts one in the form.
- **Checklist:** name, FAT, port, OLT or modem, serial, and username, updated live.

After you press **Konfirmasi**, a card in the bottom-left corner checks every 20 seconds, for up to 15 minutes, that the activation really works:

- the PPPoE session in RADIUS: online, router, IP and uptime;
- the modem on its OLT: status, PON and signal.

It ends with a verdict: working, weak signal (below −27 dBm, while the technician is still on site), or not online after 15 minutes. It also shows a snippet to copy: modem SN, name (+ village + STARLITE), username, technician, location (village, kecamatan), the KA of that village (the kepala teknis whose villages include it) and a map link. The technician and location come from the calon pelanggan record with the same name. Click any line to copy just that value (the username, the SN…); **Copy all** copies the whole snippet.

The queue shows how many days each customer has waited, and marks anything over 30 days. The helper only reads and fills the form; Konfirmasi stays yours.

### No "new version" bar

The ERP deploys many times an hour, and each deploy showed a blue "Versi baru aplikasi… Muat ulang" bar (or reloaded an idle page by itself). The script switches both off: the ERP's own "Nanti" snooze is set far ahead, and the page no longer reacts to a new service worker taking over. You get the new version whenever you reload or open a tab yourself.

### No "Aktifkan Izin Dulu" screen

When the browser has Location or Notifications blocked, the ERP covers the whole page with a dark "Aktifkan Izin Dulu" screen. The script hides it (the page keeps working and scrolling under it). Allowing both in the browser still works as before.

### Critical alarm, without the siren

The red "Alarm Insiden Kritis" screen (a router down, for example) still shows with its buttons, but its siren and vibration are muted: nothing reaches the speakers while it is up. Other sounds play as usual.

So a page never drifts far behind the server:

- **Update dot:** the build is checked every 2 minutes. When it changes, the Dock button gets a small pulsing dot.
- **Safe reload:** the page then reloads by itself once nothing is at stake: no typing in the page's fields for 10 minutes (or since you moved to another page), no dialog or Dock open, no Dock payment running, and a minute without touching anything.
- **Old-page warning:** if a page has stayed on an old build for over an hour and you save something, it asks first. Cancel stops the save so you can copy what you typed and reload.
- **No double payments:** when a Dock payment fails (for example the server restarting mid-deploy), the Dock asks the ERP what the invoice says now. A payment that went through anyway shows as Lunas; otherwise it says it's safe to try again.

### Maps for technicians

Every Dock map (Nearby, a router's offline customers, a PON port) reads as a brief:

- **Strip on top:** severity (BERAT / SEDANG / RINGAN / NORMAL), how many customers are down out of how many, the router, port or radius, the villages, and since when.
- **What each dot means:** Putus (LOS, white with a red ring), Mati listrik (dying gasp, white with a dashed grey ring), Offline (white), Redaman tinggi (online but below −28 dBm, amber), Isolir (red), Online (green). LOS, power and signal come from the OLTs' modem lists; the legend shows only what's on the map.
- **Names on the problem customers** where there's room, rings labelled in metres, a scale bar with a north arrow (U; the map never rotates, so up is always north), and the selected customer named.
- **How long they've been down:** an offline customer's name says it ("offline 2 jam"), from the drop log or the modem's last TR-069 report; a drop under 30 minutes old pulses.
- **Nearest FAT/ODP:** every down customer gets a thin line to the nearest box (within 250 m), and a box with 2 or more down shows down/all of its customers on the map ("4/6", red when half or more). A guess by distance, not the port on file.
- **Villages and teams:** where 3 or more customers live, the village is named with the field team that covers it (HRD's technician area map).
- **Unstable:** a dashed amber ring on online customers with 4 or more reconnects in 24 hours.
- **Replay:** on a router or PON map, a slider under the strip winds the map back over the last 24 hours in 15-minute steps, from the drop log's readings. It appears once the log is 15 minutes old: who's down now is placed by when they were last seen online, and those who went down and came back are added from each reading since 2.81. It covers only hours an ERP tab was open.
- **Click an empty spot** to copy its Google Maps link.
- The shared picture and note carry all of it: the note adds each village's team and a "Per FAT/ODP terdekat" line.
- **Network boxes** (FAT, ODP, FDT, ODC) from Planning's asset list, once Planning has been opened in this browser. With two or more customers down, the FAT/ODP closest to all of them is ringed as **Cek di sini**. That's where to look first, not proof.
- With customers down, the router and PON maps zoom in on them.
- **⇪ Share** copies a picture of the map with the strip, legend and scale built in, ready to paste into a chat. **Copy note** adds the text: severity, villages, the box to check with a Google Maps link, and each problem customer with phone and location. **Save PNG** saves the picture where copying isn't allowed.
- **The whole Dock moves in halftone:** the panel opens as a wave of dots from the Dock button and folds back into it; rows and cards wipe in left to right behind a dotted edge; full-screen maps open from the centre; loading lines carry a small dot scanner. The masks go once each animation ends.
- **Search loading in halftone:** while customers or invoices download, the search box shows a dot-matrix progress track with a bright scan running along it, the head dot turns into a small spinning dot radar, a faint band of dots sweeps down behind the results, and before any result arrives, ghost rows drawn in dots stand in for them. A normal search plays the same scan once (radar, a dot scan along the head line, a quicker sweep) without moving anything. When idle, the band of dots keeps sweeping slowly behind the results.
- **Motion in a halftone screen:** while the map loads, a faint dot screen with a sonar ring of brighter dots; a new view (zoom, full screen, another radius, Map/Sat) scales the old picture to where the new one sits and hands over through a halftone dissolve. Panning and data refreshes stay still. Under half a second, and off when the system asks for reduced motion.
- **Ctrl + mouse wheel** zooms in and out around the pointer (without zooming the page); drag to pan, ◎ to recentre.
- **dB** shows each online modem's redaman (from the OLTs' lists) next to its dot: white when good, amber from −26 dBm, filled amber below −28. Redaman tinggi (below −28) shows with dB off too, unless its name label already says it. The choice is remembered; past 600 modems in view only the redaman tinggi ones show until you zoom in. Not in the shared picture.
- **Sat** switches between the street map and satellite. Google's satellite tiles can't go into the picture, so a satellite share has a plain dark background.

## Incidents (3.0)

An outage becomes one **incident** with its own record, shown at the top of the Dock while it's open:

- **Outage history** (Tools, or type "history" / "riwayat"): the incidents this browser recorded (kept 90 days; 7 before Dock 3.6) for the last 7, 30 or 90 days. Router incidents that start within 15 minutes of each other count as one outage, placed with the network map at the top of the branch (or at the router above two or more failing branches). It shows outages and data stops, total time, where they start (how many times, total and longest, how many routers each took with it, when last), the time of day they start, the recent ones (each opens its incident) and the PON ports that keep failing.
- **Router incidents** open when the Outage radar sees a router well below its usual level, and close by themselves when it's back. Deeper drops (dropping → down) are logged.
- **PON incidents** open when 3 or more customers of one PON port (and a quarter of the port) go down in the same drop-log reading, and close when 80% of them are back online.
- **Desktop alerts:** switch them on once (*Desktop alerts for new incidents*). The ERP tab that took the reading sends one alert per new incident and per recovery, even with the Dock closed; clicking it opens the incident.
- **The incident card:** since when, how many affected now and at the worst, a **timeline** (what the Dock saw, plus your notes), **Add note**, **Mark resolved**. A router incident links to its router view (who's offline, cause & dispatch, report, map); a PON incident shows cause & dispatch, the report and the map right there. SPKs made and reports sent from the card are added to the timeline.
- Kept 7 days in the browser.

## Development

The script is built from `src/`: parts of the one script cut at its own section headings, joined in file-name order by `node build.mjs` into `erp-fast.user.js` (the file Tampermonkey installs). The parts aren't modules; joining them gives back the same code, so a part can use anything from another.

- Versions (`@version` in `src/000-header.js`, which Tampermonkey needs raised to update): a fix bumps the last number (3.2 → 3.2.1), a new feature the middle (3.3), a big release the first (4.0).
- `node build.mjs` builds; `node build.mjs --check` fails when the built file isn't what `src/` gives.
- `node --test` runs the tests in `test/`: the build, the router bridge's scheduling and address check, replay, down-for times and Fix RO's router matching.
- `git config core.hooksPath hooks` once, and every commit is checked; GitHub runs the same check on every push.

## Router bridge (Remote without the ERP)

`erp-router-bridge.user.js` is a second, optional userscript. With it, **Remote** opens a customer's modem page without the ERP's Remote ONU, so a wrong RO in the ERP doesn't matter: the Dock finds the session's real RO from RADIUS, and the bridge opens a temporary port on that RO itself through RouterOS 7's REST API.

- **Only for you:** the port forward accepts only your own public IP (the address the router sees on the bridge's API login), to the modem's port 80, and adds a matching forward accept only when the router has no general accept for port forwards.
- **Closes by itself:** a one-shot scheduler on the router removes the port after 30 minutes, even if your PC is off. **Close now** in the Modem box removes it earlier. Everything it adds is commented `erpdock-remote <username> <id>`.
- **Logins stay out of the page:** router logins are kept in Tampermonkey's storage for the bridge, which the ERP page and the Dock can't read. The Dock can only ask the bridge to open or close; the bridge builds the router commands, and only forwards to private (customer) addresses.
- **Set up an RO:** Tampermonkey menu → *Router bridge: set up or edit an RO*, or the button the Modem box shows the first time an RO isn't set up. You give the RO's name as the ERP writes it, its public address and API port, its NAS address (LDN → Router (NAS)), and a RouterOS user. Best is a user just for this, in a group with only the rights it needs (`read`, `write`, `rest-api`) and allowed only from your addresses.
- **https needs a valid certificate:** Tampermonkey can't accept a self-signed one. With plain `http` the router login crosses the internet unencrypted, so prefer https with a real certificate, or restrict the API service to your addresses.
- Changing router config this way is a NOC decision: set it up only with that approval.

Install it like the main script: open the raw file URL, `https://raw.githubusercontent.com/Adztrz/erp-script/main/erp-router-bridge.user.js`, and Tampermonkey offers to install it. The first time it contacts a router, Tampermonkey asks you to allow that address.

## Dock in WhatsApp Web

`erp-whatsapp.user.js` is a second, optional userscript. It puts the Dock's customer card beside the open chat in WhatsApp Web (Alt+K shows or hides it):

- **Who is writing**, found by their number, then by a username they sent in their last messages, then by the contact's name (offered to pick, not assumed). A search box finds anyone else.
- **Their state**: online, offline or isolir, plan, area, router, address, expiry, unpaid months with the total, and an open outage on their router or PON port.
- **Replies** that fill in `{nama}`, `{username}`, `{desa}`, `{bulan}`, `{tagihan}` and more. Click one and it goes into WhatsApp's message box; you send it yourself. Shift+click copies. **edit** changes them.
- **Check connection, Pay…, 1× login link** (Dock 3.18.0): done by the Dock in your open ERP tab with its own code and your sign-in. Check reads fresh sessions and gives the card's "Down?" verdict and facts; Pay loads the bills as the ERP has them now, ticks oldest first (skip older months with a note, for finance managers and admins), and pays after a second click; the 1× link is made and sent by the ERP, and goes into the chat's box if its WhatsApp gateway fails. A request carries an id the Dock answers only once, so a payment can't go twice.
- **Outages** (Dock 3.19.0): the ERP tab's radar alerts (routers down, dropping or without session data) and open PON incidents show as a red bar at the top, read each minute. Open one and the Dock works out who is offline right now: the report for the technicians, the notice for customers (into the message box) and their numbers. A customer on a router in trouble gets a line on their card that opens that outage.
- **Technicians and modem** on the card: the field team and area head for their village, the ONT (OLT, PON port, status, signal, serial), TR-069's last report, how many on the same port are offline, and **Remote** to open the modem's own page through the ERP's 5-minute port on the RO (a second click confirms; logged by the ERP).
- **Nearby** (Dock 3.20.0): the customers within 100 m, 300 m or 1 km on a map (street or satellite), online green, offline hollow (a ring pulses on those down under an hour), isolir red, with "x of y offline" and the offline ones listed nearest first; a dot or a row opens that customer. Who is online comes from the ERP tab's live sessions, read when the card opens; the map tiles are fetched by Tampermonkey.
- **Traffic** (Dock 3.21.0, also on the ERP Dock's card): the rate now against the plan's speed (from RADIUS's 5-minute reports, checked every 30 s for 12 minutes while the card is open), this session's download and upload, and everything since the customer's first session. A line used near its plan's speed is called out as full, not broken; online with hardly any traffic points at the WiFi or devices.
- **Way up** (Dock 3.22.0, also on the ERP Dock's card): the way from the customer's OLT (their modem's, else their router) up to the core, read from the ERP's router map (Network → Topology). Each link shows its traffic toward the customer now against its size. The verdict names the first trouble on the way: a router or switch that's unreachable, a link that's down, or one that's full (90%+) or busy (75%+). When everything is clear, it says the slowness is on their own line, plan or WiFi. When their modem isn't found (no ONT matches), the way starts at their router and says so: the part from the router to the house (OLT, PON port, fibre signal, neighbours on the port) isn't checked, and the modem can be found by serial in Modem / Search (3.28.0). A link the map draws backwards (a switch marked above the router feeding it) is crossed the other way and named, so the map can be fixed.
- **Above the core** (Dock 3.27.0): Way up ends with the upstream (metro + IX) from the ERP's Bandwidth page (NOC → Bandwidth, read only): its load now and the links that fill up in the busy hours. A POP's VLAN gets its real size from that page (a VLAN otherwise reports its trunk's speed, so it shows no %), with its busy-hour load. The Answer names a POP or upstream that fills up in the busy hours only around those hours; otherwise it's a "Later:" note. The radar warns when the backbone fibre toward the upstream fades or drops, and lists the upstream links that run full in the busy hours with the ERP's capacity verdict.
- **Answer** (Dock 3.23.0, also at the top of the ERP Dock's card): the card's readings joined into one cause, the next step and a reply in Indonesian to send. Offline: the way up (an area outage), then the PON port and the neighbours, then the signal (their cable), then the modem's power. Online: an uplink that's full (the area is busy), a RADIUS speed cap under the plan (the session's profile rate-limit well below the plan's Mbps, or the isolir profile on a customer the ERP has as paid: fix the PPPoE profile in RADIUS and reconnect, 3.28.0), their own line full (their use), reconnects (power or connector), hardly any traffic (WiFi or devices), else a speed test; when their modem wasn't found, "fine" says the router → house part isn't checked. Isolir gets the bill reply with the amount owed. Click the reply to put it in WhatsApp's message box; you send it.
- **Group chats** get a list of the customers writing in them, newest first: found by the sender's number (in each message's id, while WhatsApp still shows it), a username they sent, or the name your phone saved them under (two of its words in the record, one in the customer's own name). Click someone, or click a message in the chat, to open that sender's card; **Back to the group** returns to the list.
- **Open in the Dock ↗** opens that customer's card in the ERP (`#dock=<username>`, Dock 3.17.0), reusing the same tab.

WhatsApp can't read the ERP's storage, so the script runs in both places: in an ERP tab it copies what the Dock keeps (the customer list and the invoice months it has loaded) to Tampermonkey's storage once a minute when it changed, and on WhatsApp it reads that copy. Nothing is fetched from WhatsApp and nothing leaves the browser. The card is as fresh as your open ERP tab (its age shows at the top); bills show for the invoice months the Dock has loaded (Finance → Invoices once a day brings this month's).

Install it like the main script: `https://raw.githubusercontent.com/Adztrz/erp-script/main/erp-whatsapp.user.js`.

## Review queue (Approvals)

Goes through the waiting reports one after another from the keyboard. On Technical → Approvals press **Review queue** in the bar, pick a type if you like, and **Start**. Each report opens in the ERP's own review window, and the keys press that window's own controls:

| Key | Does |
|---|---|
| `C` | the window's **Centang Semua** (look at the photos first) |
| `1`–`9` | tick or untick one check |
| `Shift`+`1`–`9` | pick a reject code (on reports that have them) |
| `T` | write a note; `Enter` goes back to the keys |
| `A` `A` | **Setujui**, pressed twice so one stray key does nothing |
| `R` `R` | **Tolak & Minta Revisi**, pressed twice |
| `N` / `P` | next / previous report |
| `Esc` | close the window and pause; **Continue** picks up where you were |

After a decision is saved, by key or by clicking Setujui / Tolak yourself, the next report opens (switch that off in the panel); closing the window with Tutup pauses. A strip at the top shows where you are, who sent the report and its Validasi, and why Setujui or Tolak isn't open yet.

**Pick & approve** (the switch at the top of the panel) lists the same reports with a box each. Tick the ones to approve (**Tick all** ticks every report in the list shown, **Tick all Lengkap** only those marked Lengkap with GPS matching), press **Approve** twice, and each one is opened in the ERP's window and given **Centang Semua**; once its Setujui is open, the save that Setujui makes (the window's own, with its note "Sesuai standar.") is started and the next report is opened while it runs. Three save at once, never two of one SPK (approving one also settles its teammates' reports and the SPK), and each is checked to leave Menunggu. A report whose Setujui stays locked (checks the button doesn't tick, a step of its own) or waits for photos ("Setujui tanpa foto ini", "Foto belum sampai") is skipped and marked in the list for you to open, and so is one the ERP refuses, with its reason; anything unexpected (the save not found on the window, a save still running after 5 minutes, an approval it doesn't confirm) stops new ones. `Esc` stops starting new ones; the saves already running finish. The strip at the top counts approved, skipped and saving, and the summary at the end gives the whole time and each save's average. Only tick what you're sure of: it attests the checklist as if you had ticked it.

Nothing is decided without your keys or ticks, and nothing goes around the ERP: who may decide, all checks ticked before Setujui, and the photos being the ones you saw are still checked by the ERP as when you click. Before starting, the queue sets aside reports you may not decide and those whose photos are still on the technician's phone (the ERP's "Masih di HP"), and puts the rest in an order that keeps alike work together: Lengkap first, then by type, technician and time reported.

## Bulk SPK (Planning)

Makes SPKs from a pasted message, with no form to fill. On Planning press **Bulk SPK** in the bar and paste the message. **How to write it** under the box has an example of each kind, in a box of its own that scrolls; it's open while the box is empty. It understands both kinds of SPK, and one message can mix them:

```
Tarik & aktivasi FAT
Budi santoso
FDT DAYU ke :
* FAT DAYU 1
* FAT DAYU 2

Aktivasi FAT
Agus setiawan 10
Joko susilo 15 spk
```

- **Routes** (Tarik & Aktivasi FAT/FDT, Tarik Kabel Distribusi/Backbone, Perbaikan Kabel Backbone, Splicing Fiber: the types whose form asks for a DARI → KE route): one SPK per route. An asset line with `ke` (or a plain asset line) is where the cable starts, and the bullet lines under it are where it goes; `FDT A ke FAT B` on one line also works, from and to can be any FDT, FAT or ODP. Names are matched to Network Assets ignoring spaces and dashes; a name that isn't there yet becomes a *Calon Aset*, as in the form. A location after a name, as Google Maps copies it (`FAT DAYU 1 -8.2337, 111.3114`), is that asset's Titik Koordinat: a Calon Aset gets it, and so does an existing asset with no location saved, so the route shows on the technician's SPK map and the cable length is filled in. Or paste a Google My Maps link (shared with anyone with the link) under the heading and the technician: its FDT and FAT/ODP points are written into the box as routes with their locations, each FAT from the FDT its number names (FAT DAYU 1.4 → FDT DAYU 1, the nearest when there's none) or, when picked, from the nearest FDT; names like "FAT X 1. 2" are tidied and a name on the map twice is pointed out. Lines and areas on the map are left out. Titles follow the route template (`PENARIKAN {dari} KE {ke}` by default).
- **Counts** (Aktivasi ODP/FAT, Aktivasi FDT, Instalasi…: types with no route): `Name 10 spk` (or just `Name 10` under a heading) makes that many SPKs for that person's team, titled after the heading (`AKTIVASI FAT`). Each person's SPKs are saved in one go, so they get all of them or none.

- **Every other type in the form's Tipe Pekerjaan list** works as a count under its heading: Instalasi OLT, Instalasi Joinbox, Survey Lokasi, Maintenance Jaringan Umum, Audit Tiang Existing (needs a real project), Lain-lain. Under a `Cek port` heading, `Name 10 spk` makes *Pengecekan Port ODP (1/10)… (10/10)*, as the form does (the technician picks each ODP when reporting; 100 at most). Under an `Instalasi tiang` heading, a line like `Sudimoro 5-24` makes one SPK per pole, *Tanam Tiang wilayah Sudimoro no 5* to *no 24* (200 at most).
- Three types are left to the form, and a line for them is marked: **Migrasi HTB ke FTTH** and **Visit Pelanggan Isolir atau Dismantle** are made from customers picked in the form, and **Pemasangan CCTV HiView** is one SPK per camera with what the camera is for.

A **heading** sets the type of what follows (`Aktivasi FDT`, `Tarik kabel distribusi`…; an Aktivasi heading over routes means Tarik & Aktivasi). With no heading, routes use the panel's route type and counts are Aktivasi ODP/FAT. A **name** on its own line gives the routes under it to that technician's team (a team name, `Tim Ngadirojo 2`, works too). Routes with nobody named above them go to the team picked in the panel.

**Manual** (the switch at the top of the panel) skips the message: each row is a type, a team and how many (type part of the team's name: pick it from the list, or type any of its words in any order and press Enter), and **+ Add row** adds another. It makes the same count SPKs as above (route types aren't offered there, since they need a DARI → KE pair, nor the three form-only types).

Names are matched to employees forgivingly, but a name spelt exactly as typed wins over one that only sounds close. When several teams fit, the one named after the project's area wins, then the one where the person is a technician; if it's still unclear, or nobody fits a count line, that row shows a team picker. Pick the project (or *Bukan proyek*, as the form's checkbox) and date, and press **Create** twice.

Each SPK is the record Planning's manual form saves for a team, checked field by field against SPKs made by hand: status Baru, the team, the project, no tasks or equipment, progress 0/1, and for routes the route (with the cable length pre-filled from the straight line, as the form does) or for counts target manual. They're saved through Firestore's REST API with your own sign-in, as new documents only, then the ERP's own SPK sync (the one Misi and the SPK monitor call) is run once so they show in Daftar SPK straight away. No WhatsApp or push notice is sent; use Kirim ke WA Grup on Daftar SPK if the team should hear about them. The form's Penanggung Jawab isn't asked for: the form doesn't save it either. Team, project, route type and Paste/Manual are remembered.

## How it works

Tampermonkey lists it as running on all sites, but it only acts on the ERP: it compares the site's hostname with a stored hash and stops straight away anywhere else.


It runs before the page (`@run-at document-start`, `@grant none`) and wraps `window.fetch`:

- `GET /api/pg-read/workOrders`: lowers `limit` to the chosen amount (never raises it).
- `GET /api/pg-read/networkAssets` on `/planning`: answered from the browser's Cache Storage (`erp-fast-assets`) when the copy is under 15 minutes old.
- Fast search (LDN Radius and Invoices): it reads the page's own sign-in header from its requests (kept in memory, never saved), loads `/api/admin/ldn-radius/customers` (500 at a time) or `/api/finance/invoices/list` for the shown month (200 at a time), six requests at once (measured fastest: the server slows every page past six), re-asking for any page that comes back short, and keeps a slim copy in this browser's IndexedDB (`erp-fast`). That copy answers searches at once however old it is, while a fresh list loads behind it once it's past its time (2 hours; invoice months before last, a week). A download saves its pages as it goes, so leaving the page halfway and coming back carries on where it stopped. No passwords are loaded.

- Idle clock: real `mousedown`/`keydown`/`touchstart`/`scroll` in one tab is announced on a `BroadcastChannel` (`erp-fast-activity`) and raised as a `scroll` in the other ERP tabs, which the ERP's own idle timer listens for.

Nothing else is changed and nothing is sent anywhere.
